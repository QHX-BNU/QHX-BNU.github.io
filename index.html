04 Research
ACL 2026

One LLM Does Not Simulate All Students

Ability-Aware Student Simulation via Cognitive Diagnosis Guided LLM Assignment

Large Language Models have become integral to personalized education systems, yet most methods rely on a single high-capability LLM to represent an entire population of diverse learners. We demonstrate that this "one-size-fits-all" approach induces a systematic ability-dependent bias, and propose a framework that dynamically matches students with appropriate LLM backbones through cognitive alignment via Neural Cognitive Diagnosis.

05 Research Details

🎯 Core Problem

Current LLM-based student simulators use a single model for all learners, ignoring individual differences in ability levels. This creates systematic bias that affects the validity of educational research and system evaluation.

💡 Our Solution

We propose a novel framework that uses Neural Cognitive Diagnosis to assess student abilities, then dynamically assigns appropriate LLMs to simulate different ability levels, creating more realistic and diverse student behaviors.

🔬 Methodology

Our approach combines cognitive diagnosis models with LLM assignment strategies. We use NeuralCD to estimate student abilities from response patterns, then select LLMs with matching capability levels for simulation.

📊 Key Findings

Experiments show that our ability-aware simulation significantly reduces bias in student response patterns, achieving more realistic and diverse behavioral distributions compared to single-LLM approaches.
